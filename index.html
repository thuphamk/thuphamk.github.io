Career Journey

A strong house needs a solid foundation.

I started earning money quite early. As a student, I rented out comic books I had already read, wrote poems, and contributed short stories to the school wall newspaper. Small things, perhaps, but they were my first experiences of making something happen on my own.

My working life began in much the same way. At my first company, I was often the person running around helping with whatever needed doing in a manufacturing facility covering more than a hectare and worth tens of millions of dollars. Back then, I had no idea how much those seemingly basic tasks would shape my career. If there was work to do, I did it; if there was something to learn, I learned; if I did not know, I asked.

Over time, my work took me through logistics, commercial operations, marketing, product development, team leadership, and eventually business leadership. Every stage brought something new to learn. Some things went well; others had to be done differently the next time.

Looking back, experiences that once seemed unrelated have proved useful in ways I could not have predicted. Having worked across different functions, I tend to look beyond the part that appears to be failing and understand the wider context around it.

Selected Case Studies

Every business has its own context. Here are a few situations I have worked on, from recognizing the problem to how it was handled and what came out of it.

Mason Bees / Freshgroup

When reality changes, the strategy needs to change too.

After the first month of operations, I realized our original positioning needed to be adjusted. Once the direction changed, the work could not stop at the brand: assortment, sourcing, warehousing, sales, and customer service also needed to be reviewed.

Read the full story

Mason Bees started in 2013 with the idea “Fresh and Organic Food!” The store initially focused on around 100 SKUs, mainly vegetables and fruit, while the supply base was still limited.

After a month, what we saw in the market suggested that the original positioning was not working as expected. I worked with the team to review the assortment, sourcing, warehousing, sales, and customer service so the different parts of the business could move in the same direction rather than solve problems separately.

The assortment subsequently grew from around 100 to more than 5,000 SKUs, and average revenue per store increased around 10× across the three-store chain. The business also secured its first round of angel investment.

Looking back: When the market shows that an initial direction is not working, it is important to recognize that early and adjust the parts affected, not just the marketing message.

When I joined, the entire marketing team had left, data had been deleted, and revenue was declining. I needed to understand what was happening before deciding where to start.

Read the full story

I like beauty, although I am not exactly the most dedicated person when it comes to beauty treatments. Still, I spent a period of my career in this industry at Thien My Beauty Clinic. The challenge was not simply taking on a new role; it was helping address the business problem the owner was facing.

The marketing team was gone, the data had been deleted, revenue was falling, and internal disagreements and unstable processes were making things harder. I began by recovering whatever information was available, assessing the situation, and working through the issues one by one.

It became clear that hiring a new marketing team alone would not fix the problem. Services, pricing, the service portfolio, marketing, and operations all needed attention. While rebuilding the marketing team, I worked with colleagues to adjust the areas affecting business performance.

Over time, revenue reached nearly three times the level when I took over at its peak, while profit doubled.

Looking back: The place where a problem becomes visible is not always where it begins. Before asking one function to work harder, understand what is holding back the overall result.

03
Tami Nature Home

From contract manufacturing to a consumer-facing brand.

10,000+mouth-spray units in the first 3 months on e-commerce platforms

Tami began as an OEM/ODM cosmetics manufacturer. The goal was to build its own brand and bring selected products closer to end consumers.

Read the full story

When I joined the project, the owner wanted to develop a proprietary brand and move strong products from wholesale toward direct consumer demand. However, some existing cosmetics lines did not fully fit the preferences of consumers in major cities.

Together with colleagues, I reviewed brand positioning, packaging, and the product assortment, while also considering slow-moving lines. Alongside traditional channels, we developed e-commerce.

Market research led us to develop a mouth spray aimed at Gen Z: energetic, distinctive, convenient for everyday use, and easy to carry. The design, packaging, pricing, and sales approach were all considered for this audience. The product sold more than 10,000 units in its first three months on e-commerce platforms.

Looking back: A new product needs more than attractive packaging. You need to understand who will buy it, what they need, and how you will reach them.

04
ASPO UNIVERSE

Entering a new market where marketing and product experience must work together.

Top 10App Store, RPG downloads
18,000+player accounts

GameFi was still relatively new when I joined ASPO UNIVERSE, a project under Gosu Corporation. Marketing was not only about promoting the product; it also required close collaboration with the development team to shape the player experience.

Read the full story

Online gaming was already popular in Vietnam, but GameFi was still unfamiliar to many people at the time. I chose to take on a new environment where there was a lot to learn, learning as I went.

Together with the marketing team, I helped build the gaming community and worked with developers on characters, storylines, and gameplay. I also planned budgets and coordinated online, offline, and in-game activities.

The app reached the App Store Top 10 for role-playing game downloads, with more than 10,000 downloads in its first week. At a later stage, the platform recorded more than 18,000 player accounts and around 7,000 daily active users. The team maintained at least three community activities each month.

Looking back: For digital products, marketing needs to work alongside product development and the user community. Promotion may bring people in, but the experience is a major reason they stay.

05
Saigon Security

Redesigning how a business operates.

100%tender win rate across the tenders entered

Security services operate on tight margins and high labor costs. The challenge is to maintain service quality, stay competitive, and balance the economics of the business with employees' livelihoods.

Read the full story

As CEO of Saigon Security, I have to address several priorities at once: the company needs competitive pricing, clients need reliable service quality, and employees need sustainable work and income.

I reviewed company policies, operating processes, the organizational chart, and job descriptions. Compensation was redesigned to be more flexible and linked to revenue, helping the company respond to business conditions while taking employees' income and livelihoods into account. I also reviewed service costing and compensation policies for security guards, the people who directly deliver service quality every day.

On the business development side, I personally participated in tenders for major corporate clients, with a 100% win rate among the bids I took part in.

Looking back: Organization, processes, service costing, compensation, and business development affect one another. In leadership, they need to be considered together rather than optimized in isolation.
